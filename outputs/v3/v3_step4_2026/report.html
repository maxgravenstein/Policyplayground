8. Limits

  • Static. No change in work, saving, or migration, except the rough adjustment of the behavior module (spec 7.4), and only when it is on.
  • One year. No transition path over many years. (A separate spreadsheet model covers 2027-2070.)
  • Missing income. Survey data under-reports high incomes and capital gains. The data have no employer retirement contributions. Inheritances are part of the design but are not in the data: they are excluded.
  • Corporate tax is an estimate outside the microsimulation: flat_rate x corporate_profit_base. The microsimulation taxes dividends and capital gains in full and does not model the shareholder credit.
  • No spending outside taxes and transfers. Defense, interest, and other federal functions are not modeled.
  • SSI and Medicaid interactions. PolicyEngine leaves `basic_income` out of SSI, SNAP and Medicaid income while it is not taxable. So the UBI does not reduce SSI or Medicaid in this model (config: ubi.counts_as_ssi_income). Under current SSI rules, a cash UBI would count as unearned income.
  • State taxes stay at current law. They still use federal AGI and the federal EITC, so they do not change much. Their MTR is in the reform MTR.
  • Net income is PolicyEngine household net income plus ACA premium tax credits (cash-like). Medicaid, CHIP and Medicare are not in net income.
  • Spec v2 approximations. The 5-year wait for permanent residents is not in the data (all are eligible). Employer health premiums are imputed (Populace has none): average employer share for workers with employer coverage. The simple EITC uses PolicyEngine's EITC formula (phase-out on income, not earnings only) with full take-up. TANF, child care, Head Start and LIHEAP do not yet count the UBI. The unemployment top-up is approximated as half of today's benefit. SSDI family members, the SSDI earnings taper, inheritances, the owner credit for profit kept in pass-through businesses, the capital gains basis adjustment, loss carry-forward and home-sale indexing are not modeled.
  • Poverty at any rate. In spec v2 nothing but the flat tax depends on the rate, so SPM resources are recomputed exactly at the revenue-neutral rate (checked against PolicyEngine at the run rate).
  • Employer payroll tax. The reform ends it. Workers get the saving as higher wages (the usual CBO and JCT assumption), in full and at once. In real life pay may take a few years to adjust. The higher wages are in the flat tax base and count as earnings for benefits.
  • Data. Populace US 2024 (build populace-us-2024-spm-20260909, pinned by policyengine-us 2.107.4), survey year 2024, aged to 2026 by PolicyEngine. Baseline totals differ from published totals; see the checks table.
  • Employer health premiums. The config puts them in the tax base, but this dataset has no employer health premiums, so they are left out (spec 2.2: 'if the dataset has them'). The base is smaller and the revenue-neutral rate higher than with them.
  • Not computed. medicaid: the simulation needed more memory than this machine has. These budget lines are blank and left out of the totals. They are programs kept at current law, so the reform changes them little.
